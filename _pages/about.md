*I am a scientist eager to work on problems related to complex chronic illnesses and their comorbidities.*

After being diagnosed with hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (hEDS), I am motivated to pursue a career in complex chronic illness-related research and disability advocacy. I have seen firsthand the gaps in our understanding of chronic illnesses and their comorbidities, the lack of resources for chronically ill individuals, and the urgent need for systems that center lived experience and collaborative, patient-informed approaches to care and research. These challenges drive me to engage in patient-led research as a prospective graduate student, with the goal of improving patient outcomes and expanding resources for the disabled community. My research interests center on uncovering the fundamental mechanisms that contribute to the pathophysiology of complex chronic illnesses and their comorbidities, with a focus on the molecular, structural, and immunological processes that underlie polygenic multisystem diseases.

Currently, I am working on questions related to the co-evolution of life and the environment using quantitative analyses of microbialite morphology in Dr. Akshay Mehra’s Reconstructions Lab in the <a href='https://www.ess.uw.edu/'>Department of Earth and Space Sciences</a> at the University of Washington. Previously, I concentrated on problems related to climate feedbacks in wetland ecosystems using biogeochemical analyses to measure microbially mediated greenhouse gas fluxes in Dr. Mari Winkler’s <a href='https://winklerlab.com/'>research group</a> in the <a href='https://www.ce.washington.edu/'>Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering</a>.

Before transitioning into geoscience, I worked as a research associate at <a href="https://rarecyte.com/">RareCyte, Inc.</a>, focusing on the design and optimization of highly multiplexed immunofluorescence staining and imaging methods, and development and validation of clinical circulating tumor and rare cell biomarker assays. Prior to this role, I was a lab assistant in the molecular oncology department at <a href="https://cellnetix.com/">CellNetix Pathology & Laboratories, LLC</a>, where I characterized cancer subtypes to support diagnosis, prognosis, and drug compatibility assessments for patients.

At an earlier stage in my academic journey, I studied music performance at <a href="https://college.berklee.edu/">Berklee College of Music</a>, where I was awarded a full-tuition, merit-based scholarship. While pursuing my education in music, I studied and performed with MacArthur Fellow and Pulitzer Prize recipient Tyshawn Sorey, MacArthur Fellow Vijay Iyer, and MacArthur Fellow Jason Moran, amongst others.

As a first-generation student, I am committed to mentoring students with underrepresented identities in STEM and advancing equitable access to research and educational opportunities. My advocacy experience includes leading the community research arm of <a href='https://kingcountyequitynow.com/'>King County Equity Now</a>, where I collaborated with Seattle City Council to drive legislative action and fund participatory budgeting programs for local BIPOC communities. Additionally, I organized with the <a href='https://www.realrentduwamish.org/about-the-duwamish-solidarity-group.html'>Duwamish Solidarity Group</a> to lobby for increased recognition and support of the Duwamish Tribe and preservation of historical sites in the greater Seattle area.
