*I am a researcher eager to work on problems related to hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (hEDS) and its comorbidities.*

After being diagnosed with hEDS, I am driven to pursue a career in hEDS-related research and disability advocacy. I have seen firsthand the gaps in our understanding of hEDS and its comorbidities, the lack of resources for chronically ill individuals, and the urgent need for systems that center lived experience and collaborative, patient-informed approaches to care and research. These challenges motivate me to engage in patient-led research as a prospective graduate student, with the goal of improving patient outcomes and expanding resources for the EDS community. My research interests center on uncovering the fundamental mechanisms that drive the pathophysiology of hEDS and its comorbidities, with a focus on the molecular, structural, and immunological processes that underlie this complex multisystem disorder.

Previously, I worked on questions related to the co-evolution of life and the environment and climate feedbacks in wetland ecosystems as a geobiologist and biogeochemist. While working in these fields, I was a member of Dr. Akshay Mehra’s Reconstructions Lab in the <a href='https://www.ess.uw.edu/'>Department of Earth and Space Sciences</a> and Dr. Mari Winkler’s <a href='https://winklerlab.com/'>research group</a> in the <a href='https://www.ce.washington.edu/'>Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering</a> as a post-baccalaureate student at the University of Washington.

Before transitioning into geoscience, I worked as a research associate at <a href="https://rarecyte.com/">RareCyte, Inc.</a>, focusing on the design and optimization of highly multiplexed immunofluorescence staining and imaging methods and development and validation of clinical circulating tumor and rare cell biomarker assays. Prior to working as a research associate, I was a lab assistant in the molecular oncology department at <a href="https://cellnetix.com/">CellNetix Pathology & Laboratories, LLC</a>, where I characterized cancer subtypes for diagnosis, prognosis, and assessment of drug compatibility for cancer patients.

At an earlier stage in my academic journey, I studied music performance at <a href="https://college.berklee.edu/">Berklee College of Music</a>, where I was awarded a full-tuition, merit-based scholarship. While pursuing my education in music, I studied and performed with MacArthur Fellow and Pulitzer Prize recipient Tyshawn Sorey, MacArthur Fellow Vijay Iyer, and MacArthur Fellow Jason Moran, amongst others.

As a first-generation student, I am committed to mentoring students with underrepresented identities in STEM and advancing equitable access to research and educational opportunities. My advocacy experience includes leading the research arm of King County Equity Now, where I collaborated with Seattle City Council to drive legislative action and fund participatory budgeting programs for BIPOC communities. Additionally, I organized with the Duwamish Solidarity Group to lobby for increased recognition of the Duwamish Tribe and preservation of historical sites.
